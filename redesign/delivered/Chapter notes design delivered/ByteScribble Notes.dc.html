01 Reflection of light

A polished surface sends light back into the same medium instead of absorbing it. This bouncing back is reflection, and it obeys two rules that never change — whether the surface is a plane mirror or a curved one.

Definition

Laws of reflection

(i) The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection. (ii) The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.

Key points — image in a plane mirror

  • ✓Virtual and erect.
  • ✓Same size as the object.
  • ✓As far behind the mirror as the object is in front.
  • ✓Laterally inverted — left becomes right.

02 Spherical mirrors

A spherical mirror is a slice of a sphere. If the reflecting surface curves inward it is concave; outward, convex. Almost every question here is answered by locating the object relative to the focus F and the centre of curvature C.

Formula Mirror formula
1v + 1u = 1f
m= h′h =− vu

When to use. Any numerical giving two of u, v, f for a mirror. Distances are measured from the pole, with the incident direction positive.

Object position Image Size
Beyond C Real, inverted Diminished
At C Real, inverted Same size
Between F and C Real, inverted Enlarged
Between P and F Virtual, erect Enlarged

03 Refraction of light

Light changes speed when it enters a new medium, so it changes direction at the boundary. That is why a pencil looks bent in a glass of water and a coin in a bucket appears raised.

Definition

Refractive index

The ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to its speed in the medium. A higher refractive index means light slows more, so it bends more. Glass ≈ 1.5, water ≈ 1.33.

Formula Snell's law
sin isin r = n21 = v₁v₂

When to use. Whenever a ray crosses from one medium to another and you know either the angles or the refractive indices.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring angles from the surface instead of from the normal.
  • Forgetting the minus sign for an object distance u — it is always negative in the CBSE convention.
  • Writing f positive for a concave mirror. Concave mirror and convex lens: f negative and positive respectively.

04 Lenses & power

Formula Lens formula & power
1v − 1u = 1f P= 1f (dioptre, f in m)

When to use. Lens numericals; and power whenever the question mentions dioptres or spectacle lenses.

Solved example

Problem. An object is placed 25 cm from a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. Find the position of the image.

  1. 1Write what is given with signs: u = −25 cm, f = +10 cm.
  2. 2Use the lens formula: 1/v = 1/f + 1/u.
  3. 3Substitute: 1/v = 1/10 − 1/25 = (5 − 2)/50 = 3/50.
  4. 4Solve: v = 50/3 ≈ 16.7 cm. Positive, so the image is on the far side of the lens — real and inverted.

Answer. The image forms 16.7 cm behind the lens; real, inverted, diminished.

Formula

Magnification: m = h′/h; mirror −v/u, lens v/u.

Definition

Refractive index — speed of light in vacuum ÷ speed in the medium.

Values

Water 1.33 · glass ≈ 1.5 · diamond 2.42

Watch out

u is always negative. Concave mirror f < 0; convex lens f > 0.

Key point

Convex mirror & concave lens always give a virtual, erect, diminished image.
